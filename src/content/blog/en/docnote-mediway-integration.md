Electronic health records only help when documentation lands where clinicians already work. That is why DocNote reports can now integrate directly into the Mediway EHR through a feature developed with Mediway. Physicians record and structure a consultation with DocNote, then import the report into a Mediway consultation in a single click, without extra manipulation.

The goal is practical. Save physician time. Keep documentation reliable. Fit Mediway users’ existing habits instead of asking them to invent a parallel workflow.

## Why EHR import is the real adoption test

Many AI documentation tools demonstrate well in a sandbox. The draft looks clean. The demo feels fast. Then the pilot starts and clinicians discover a hidden tax: they still have to move text into the record by hand, fix formatting, or re-enter fields the EHR expects.

That last mile decides whether a tool becomes daily practice or a short experiment. For Mediway users, one-click import removes that friction. The consultation stays in the environment they already open between patients. DocNote becomes part of the visit close-out, not a side application that produces orphaned files.

This matters for ambulatory and hospital teams evaluating an [AI medical scribe](/ai-medical-scribe/). Speech-to-structure is useful. Speech-to-structure-into-the-EHR is what changes the day.

## How the DocNote and Mediway workflow works

The joint feature keeps the sequence short and familiar.

1. The physician conducts the consultation as usual.
2. DocNote records and automatically structures the clinical content into a medical report.
3. With one click, the report integrates into a Mediway consultation.
4. The physician reviews inside the EHR context, adjusts if needed, and continues with normal Mediway completion steps.

There is no requirement to rebuild daily habits around a new note editor. The design assumption is simple: Mediway remains the system of record. DocNote accelerates how content is produced and handed over.

What “no additional manipulation” means in practice:

- No retyping the visit narrative from memory
- No multi-step export chains that invite version confusion
- No hunting for the latest draft in email or a desktop folder
- Fewer copy-paste errors that strip headings or lists
- Faster same-day closure when the schedule is full

Teams that already use DocNote for structured reports can treat Mediway import as the natural next step once their site enables the feature.

## Designed for Mediway users, not against their habits

Integration projects fail when vendors insist that clinicians “just switch tools.” DocNote’s Mediway work takes the opposite approach. It respects the screens, consultation objects, and closing rituals physicians already know.

That product choice has clinical and operational consequences:

- Training time stays shorter because the destination UI is familiar
- Change management focuses on when to start recording, not how to abandon the EHR
- Audit and chart review still happen where compliance teams expect them
- Cross-cover colleagues open the same Mediway record they always did

For organizations standardizing [hospital documentation](/hospital-documentation/) across services, EHR-native landing of AI drafts is often a procurement requirement, not a nice-to-have. Mediway users now have a concrete answer for that requirement.

## Reliability gains beyond raw minutes saved

Time savings are easy to advertise. Reliability is harder and often more valuable. When physicians rewrite notes under time pressure, details drift. Medication changes get vague. Follow-up instructions shorten. Laterality and chronology blur.

A structured DocNote draft imported into Mediway reduces those failure modes by capturing content closer to the encounter and placing it into the chart without a second transcription step. The physician still validates clinical accuracy. What disappears is the high-error handoff between “I said it” and “I typed it an hour later.”

Reliability improvements teams often notice:

- More complete problem lists reflected in the visit narrative
- Clearer linkage between assessment and plan
- Fewer forgotten counseling points at the end of a busy session
- More consistent section structure across physicians in the same practice
- Better continuity when a colleague opens the chart the next day

These effects support patient safety and team coordination even when the stopwatch improvement looks modest on paper.

## Rolling out DocNote with Mediway in a practice or hospital unit

A clean technical feature still needs a calm operational plan. Treat the Mediway import as a workflow change with a short pilot, not a silent switch.

Suggested rollout:

1. Confirm Mediway feature availability with your local Mediway and IT contacts.
2. Choose a pilot group of physicians who already finish charts the same day when possible.
3. Align which DocNote document types you will import first (for example standard consultation reports).
4. Define the review checklist before signature (meds, allergies, follow-up, critical negatives).
5. Collect two weeks of feedback on clicks, edit time, and any field mapping surprises.
6. Expand to additional clinics once the first group would not go back.

During the pilot, keep support paths obvious. Physicians should know who to contact for Mediway-side questions versus DocNote-side questions. Ambiguous ownership is a common reason integrations stall after the announcement.

### Change management without theater

Technical import is only half the story. Practices and hospital units still need a light change plan so the feature does not become “that button nobody uses.”

Keep communication concrete. Tell physicians exactly when to start recording, when to import, and what they must still verify. Avoid abstract AI education days that never touch a real chart. A twenty-minute huddle with two live examples usually outperforms a long slide deck.

Involve medical assistants and secretarial teams early when they participate in chart closure. If their role shifts from typing full letters to polishing structured imports, say so. Ambiguity about who edits what creates duplicate work and quiet resistance.

Also plan for the mixed period when some physicians use DocNote with Mediway and others do not. Chart readers should still find a consistent minimum structure. Alignment on headings before the pilot prevents a month of stylistic arguments after go-live.

## What Mediway physicians can do next

Physicians using Mediway can discover and try DocNote directly on the DocNote website. Start with a realistic visit type you do often. Do not begin with the most complex multidisciplinary case on the schedule. Prove the loop on ordinary consultations, then stretch to harder notes.

Evaluation checklist for the first week:

- Does one-click import place content where you expect inside Mediway?
- How many minutes do you spend editing versus typing from scratch?
- Does the structured layout match how your practice writes notes?
- Can you finish more charts before leaving the clinic?
- Would you keep the workflow if the pilot ended tomorrow?

If those answers are positive, involve your practice manager or hospital IT early so scaling does not depend on informal workarounds.
